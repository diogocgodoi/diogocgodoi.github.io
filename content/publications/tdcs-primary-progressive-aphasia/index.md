abstract: "Introduction: Transcranial Direct Current Stimulation (tDCS) has shown promising language improvements in patients with primary progressive aphasia (PPA). Yet, individual studies have not been sufficient to yield strong conclusions on its efficacy. Methods: We performed a systematic review and meta-analysis of randomised controlled trials (RCTs) comparing tDCS against sham stimulation in patients with PPA. We searched PubMed, Embase, and Cochrane Central databases for eligible studies up to July 2024. Outcomes of interest included performance in a range of language and cognitive tests. Summary data were extracted from published reports and pooled using a random-effects model with standardized mean differences (SMDs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs). Results: We included 10 parallel and cross-over RCTs with 178 patients and 218 observations. tDCS yielded significant improvements in general naming (SMD 0.37; 95% CI 0.07–0.67) and spelling ability (SMD 0.65; 95% CI 0.10–1.20). There were no significant differences for trained or untrained naming items, global language, working memory, semantic fluency, or comprehension. Conclusion: tDCS may improve general naming and spelling ability in patients with PPA, although evidence for effects on other language and cognitive functions remains limited."
