Behavioural and Genetic Predictors of Positive Symptoms in Psychotic Disorders

June 1, 2026·
*Godoi, D. C.
,
Walters, J. T.
,
Legge, S. E.
· 0 min read
Abstract
Introduction: Schizophrenia and related psychotic disorders are characterised by positive, negative and disorganised symptoms. This study investigated whether clinical, social and genetic factors are associated with the severity of individual positive psychotic symptoms. Methods: A sample of 1,297 individuals with an ICD-10 diagnosis of schizophrenia or a related psychotic disorder was assessed using the Scale for Assessment of Positive Symptoms. Clinical and social predictors were examined using univariate linear regressions, while polygenic risk scores for schizophrenia, bipolar disorder, ADHD, autism and intelligence were tested for associations with positive symptoms. Results: Earlier age of onset, cannabis use and tobacco smoking were associated with greater severity of multiple hallucinations and delusions. Premorbid IQ and educational attainment were positively associated with several types of delusions but negatively associated with global and auditory hallucinations. Childhood abuse was associated with greater severity of auditory, visual and somatosensory hallucinations. The only significant polygenic risk score association was between intelligence polygenic risk and religious delusions. Conclusions: Clinical and environmental factors showed symptom-specific associations with positive psychotic symptoms, while genetic liability showed limited associations. These findings highlight the heterogeneity of positive symptoms in psychotic disorders.
